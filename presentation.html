05 — Algorithm I

Personalized PageRank
toward AC seed nodes

PPR_AC — Steady-state equation
π = α·p + (1−α)·WT·π
where pi = 1/|AC| if authori ∈ AC members, else 0
α = 0.85  ·  W = normalized co-authorship weight matrix
wuv = count(shared papers) / min(papers_u, papers_v)
Intuition: A random walker on the co-authorship graph restarts toward AC members with probability (1−α) at each step. The steady-state probability of finding the walker at author i directly measures "how much AC influence structurally flows through this person" — regardless of how many papers they've published.
Why not plain PageRank?

Standard PageRank treats all nodes as equal seeds. PPR_AC seeds only from AC members, so it captures proximity to AC's actual research community — not overall network prominence.

Why normalized edge weights?

A senior PI co-authoring 200 papers would dominate unweighted graphs. Normalizing by min(deg_u, deg_v) discounts co-authorship that is explained by productivity.

What it answers

Which researchers — perhaps not yet AC-funded — sit structurally close to AC's knowledge frontier? Who should be on our radar next?

06 — Algorithm II

Evans & Chen normalized centrality
separating importance from productivity

Normalized closeness — Evans & Chen (2022), extended
c̃r(kr) = 1 β − ln(kr)ln(z̄)

cnorm(r) = cactual(r) c̃r(kr)
kr = degree (distinct collaborators)  ·  z̄ = mean degree
β calibrated from the empirical degree-closeness fit of the full graph
Our extension: weighted harmonic centrality (Opsahl 2010) on 1/w distances

In most networks, closeness is largely explained by degree. Evans & Chen show that the residual — actual closeness vs. degree-predicted closeness — is what reveals truly strategic nodes.

c > 1 Bridge node. More central than its collaboration count predicts. Cross-SDL connector, interdisciplinary pivot. BRIDGE
c ≈ 1 Centrality fully explained by productivity. Prominent, but not strategically exceptional.
c < 1 Structurally peripheral relative to their paper count. Large producer in a silo. SILO
07 — Algorithm III · The Combined Score

Bridge Score — the crown metric

bridge(r) = PPRAC(r) × [1 + λ · max(0, cnorm(r) − 1)] × diversity(r)
λ = 0.5  ·  diversity = log(1 + |SDL topics connected|) / log(1 + max_topics)  ·  score ∈ [0, 1]
PPR_AC
Structural proximity to AC's research community via the co-authorship graph. Captures latent influence flow.
Evans & Chen boost
Amplifies nodes that are more central than their productivity predicts. Demotes prolific silos. Rewards true bridges.
Cross-SDL diversity
Log-scaled count of SDL topics connected. Identifies researchers linking AI decision-making, automation, and closed-loop control.
High bridge score = AC-adjacent + structurally exceptional + cross-disciplinary. Not just "who published most" — but "who holds the field together."

From RAG to
structured knowledge pages

Knowledge persistence model
Every query re-reads raw text. No memory of previous reasoning. Classification results thrown away.
Each paper/author/institution gets a structured wiki page. Classification rationale stored permanently. Future queries start from compiled knowledge.
Dual-model disagreement = human reviews and forgets the outcome.
Disagreement + human label + rationale → permanent rule asset. Lint checks enforce it automatically on all future papers.
Accuracy depends on freshness of retrieval context.
Accuracy depends on quality of maintained knowledge base. Improves over time without re-reading everything.
