## Abstract
The depletion of traditional fossil fuels and growing energy concerns have led to the development of efficient, carbon-neutral energy conversion technologies powered by renewable sources. Among these, green hydrogen production through electrochemical water splitting is widely considered a promising strategy for energy storage. The proton exchange membrane (PEM) system in various water electrolysis technologies is particularly noteworthy for its high current densities, excellent voltage efficiency, and minimal hydrogen cross-diffusion, bringing it closer to commercialization. However, the efficiency of water splitting is constrained by the slow oxygen evolution reactions (OER), the anodic reaction in the process. Artificial intelligence (AI) is revolutionizing numerous fields, including science and technology, by streamlining complex tasks. High-throughput experiments (HTE), aided by AI, provide an accelerated pathway for catalyst discovery, overcoming the challenges posed by traditional, time-consuming experimental methods. Metal oxide catalysts are attractive for OER due to their low-cost transition metals, high structural stability, and element substitutability. However, the reaction mechanisms of oxides, including reaction pathways, kinetic barriers, and surface reconstruction, highlight the need to explore their structure-performance relationships for deeper insights. The complex chemical space offers countless potential materials for synthesis, which cannot be addressed through traditional reliance on heuristics and extensive testing. Researchers are increasingly utilizing AI to predict material properties, enabling the design of new materials by navigating chemical space. Given the complexity of optimizing materials with multidimensional compositions (potentially exceeding > 200,000 candidates based on pyrochlores), novel experimental methods are essential for advancing effective OER catalysts. This work reveals the relationship among catalyst synthesis, reaction mechanisms, and performance (activity/stability) by establishing a combined AI-HTE platform, which includes a self-built database, a state-of-the-art AI model, and high-throughput (HT) experiments/ calculations. Meanwhile, for the first time, this work successfully implement AI to achieve green hydrogen production and drive renewable energy storage and transition.
