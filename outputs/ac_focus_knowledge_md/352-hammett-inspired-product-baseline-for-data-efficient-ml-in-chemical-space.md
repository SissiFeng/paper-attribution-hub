Data-hungry machine learning methods have become a new standard to efficiently navigate chem-
ical compound space for molecular and materials design and discovery. Due to the severe scarcity
and cost of high-quality experimental or synthetic simulated training data, however, data-acquisition
costs can be considerable. Relying on reasonably accurate approximate legacy baseline labels with
low computational complexity represents one of the most effective strategies to curb data-needs,
e.g. through ∆-, transfer-, or multi-fidelity learning. A surprisingly effective and data-efficient base-
line model is presented in the form of a generic coarse-graining Hammett-Inspired Product (HIP)
